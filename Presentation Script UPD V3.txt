Slide 6 — Objective 1 Model Development (5:45–8:00)
Aayush

"We evaluated statistical significance, VIF values, AIC, BIC, and diagnostic plots."

Aayush

"We wanted to understand which hospital characteristics were associated with longer stays, not simply maximize prediction accuracy."

Josh

"One major issue we discovered was severe multicollinearity involving Beds, AvgDailyCensus, and AvgNurses."

Aayush

"In particular, Beds and AvgDailyCensus had a correlation of approximately 0.98."

"When predictors are that highly correlated, they are essentially providing the model with the same information."

"This can inflate standard errors and make coefficient estimates unstable."

Josh

"Because Beds was also statistically insignificant, we removed it and retained Average Daily Census."

Aayush

"We then removed Routine Culture Ratio, Medical School Affiliation, and Percent Services Facilities because they did not provide strong evidence of association with length of stay."

Josh

"After comparing candidate models using AIC, BIC, adjusted R-squared, and VIF values, we selected our reduced model as the final Objective 1 model."

Slide 11 — Prediction Results

Josh

Aayush

“It achieved the lowest RMSE at 1.2315 days and the highest cross-validated R².”

Josh

“The interaction model achieved a slightly lower MAE, but the improvement was extremely small relative to the additional complexity.”

Aayush

“Both regression models outperformed the Random Forest model, likely because of the relatively small sample size of only 113 hospitals.”

Aayush

"Many people expect machine learning methods to automatically outperform regression models."

Josh

"However, our dataset only contained 113 hospitals."

Aayush

Josh

"This result reinforces the idea that additional complexity does not always improve predictive performance."
